**ECON1626 — Economics of Artificial Intelligence | Assessment 2**  
**Author:** Jackson Gill  
**Date:** May 2026

## Overview

This repository contains the final policy proposal and iterative drafts for ECON1626 Assessment 2. The brief is addressed to the Deputy Secretary of Employment & Skills and analyses how artificial intelligence is restructuring the labour market through task automation, augmentation, and data concentration. It evaluates three policy options against explicit economic criteria and recommends a phased implementation package.

## Policy Brief Summary

The proposal argues that AI is disrupting labour markets through three channels — routine task automation, augmentation of high-skill workers, and data-driven market concentration — and that the reinstatement mechanism operating in prior technological waves is weaker for AI (*so-so automation*). It evaluates:

- **Option 1:** National Reskilling Fund (addresses human capital underinvestment)
- **Option 2:** Labour Market Adjustment Mechanisms (advance notification, short-time work, wage insurance)
- **Option 3:** Competition & Data Policy Reform (data-sharing obligations, merger review)

The recommended package sequences all three in phases across Years 1–5, with responsible agencies, cost estimates, and measurable KPIs defined for each phase.
